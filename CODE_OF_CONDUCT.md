# Code of conduct

[日本語](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.ja.md)

Drag'n Wash is a first-person washing game with a story: you play a kobold who
runs a wash service, and the three dragons who are your customers get to know
you as the levels go on. This is the small project where people from a dozen
language communities put all of that into their own language. Treat it as the
friendly place it is.

## What is expected

- **Be kind, and assume the other person means well.** Nearly everyone here is
  doing this for fun, in a spare hour, in a language that is not their first.
  Read the friendliest version of what they wrote.
- **Be patient with beginners.** "How do I even open a pull request" is a fine
  question, and so is a CSV that came out with the columns in the wrong order.
  Everyone translating here today got something wrong the first time.
- **Keep criticism about the work, and be specific.** "Ryan would not use a
  polite form here, he is teasing Conrad" is useful. "Your English is bad" is
  not.
- **A translation is a judgement call, not a right answer.** Two native
  speakers can disagree about a line and both be right. Argue about the line,
  not about who is the more real speaker of the language.
- **Take a hint.** If someone asks you to drop a topic or change how you are
  talking to them, drop it.

## What is not

- Harassment of any kind, in issues, pull requests, commit messages, code,
  translations or anywhere else this project reaches
- Insults or attacks about who someone is, rather than what they wrote —
  including about their nationality, language, accent, ability, gender,
  sexuality, religion, age or appearance. "You are not a real native speaker"
  as a way to win an argument belongs here.
- Unwanted sexual attention aimed at a person here. The game is an adult one,
  so explicit lines in the CSVs, and a serious discussion of how to translate
  one, are the work. A remark aimed at a contributor rather than at the text is
  not, and neither is bringing that register into a thread about something else
- Slipping something into a translation that is not in the game: slurs, jokes
  at someone's expense, politics, advertising, or anything the original line
  does not say. The game's words are not yours to change.
- Publishing someone's private information, or threatening to
- Sustained disruption: reopening settled arguments, spamming, deliberately
  wasting a reviewer's time
- Posting story spoilers outside the files that are meant to hold them — the
  CSVs and the issues about them. Not everyone reading an issue has finished
  the game.

## Where this applies

Everywhere the project happens: issues, pull requests, discussions, commits,
translated text itself, release notes, and any place where someone is speaking
for this project or its community.

## Reporting

If someone is out of line, tell the maintainer ([@TomXV](https://github.com/TomXV)).

- For anything you would rather not post in public, use the private form under
  the repository's **Security** tab —
  [**Report a vulnerability**](https://github.com/TomXV/dragnwash-localization/security/advisories/new).
  It is meant for security reports, but it is the private channel this
  repository has, and it reaches the same person. Say at the top that it is a
  conduct report, not a security one.
- Anything else can be an ordinary issue.
- If the problem is with the maintainer, or you want someone else entirely,
  use GitHub's own [report abuse](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam)
  form. GitHub staff are not involved in this project.

Reports are read in private. You will get a reply. Depending on what happened,
the answer may be a quiet word, an edited or deleted comment, a reverted
translation, a closed thread, or a block from the repository. Deliberately
false reports are themselves a breach of this document.
